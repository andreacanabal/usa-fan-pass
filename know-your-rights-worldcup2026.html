The 5 phrases that protect you

  • 1
    "Am I free to go?"
    →
    If yes: leave calmly. If no: you are being detained.
  • 2
    "I am invoking my right to remain silent."
    →
    Say this clearly. Then stop talking. Protected by the 5th Amendment.
  • 3
    "I do not consent to a search."
    →
    Say it even if they search anyway. It preserves your legal rights.
  • 4
    "I want to speak to a lawyer before answering questions."
    →
    Once you say this, questioning must stop by law.
  • 5
    "I am a foreign national. I request consular notification."
    →
    Under the Vienna Convention, you have the right to contact your embassy.

The Golden Rule

Be calm. Be polite. Do not run. Do not argue. Do not physically resist.

Your goal is to end the interaction safely — not to win an argument.

What police CAN do

✓ Ask your name (in most states you must provide it)
✓ Ask what you are doing
✓ Pat down outer clothing if they suspect a weapon
✓ Detain you temporarily with "reasonable suspicion"
✓ Arrest you if they have "probable cause"

What police CANNOT do

✗ Search your belongings without consent or a warrant (with some exceptions)
✗ Hold you indefinitely without charging you
✗ Deny you consular access as a foreign national
✗ Use excessive force

Passport & documents

✓ DO carry a photocopy of your passport + entry stamp (keep original in hotel safe)
✗ DO NOT hand your passport to anyone except official immigration or police
✓ A photocopy is generally acceptable ID in most states

🚨 If your passport is confiscated

1. Get a receipt with officer's name and badge number.
2. Contact your embassy immediately (numbers in the Embassies tab).
3. Your embassy can issue an emergency travel document within 24–48 hours.
4. Do NOT leave the country on a confiscated passport — this can be a crime.

📍 If your passport is lost or stolen

1. File a police report — get the report number.
2. Contact your embassy immediately.
3. Go to nearest consulate in person with: police report + 2 passport photos + any ID you have.

Your Filming Rights

Filming police in public is a First Amendment right in all 50 states. However, each state has nuances — know the rules for your host city.

By host city / state

🗽 New York
  • Legal to film police in public spaces
  • Stay on public property at all times
  • Do not interfere with the officer's duties
  • If ordered to move back, comply while continuing to film
🌴 California (Los Angeles)
  • Strong filming rights — well-established case law
  • Recent law: stay 8+ feet away if ordered by officer
  • Do not narrate loudly while filming — obstruction charges have been upheld
  • Back footage to cloud immediately after filming
⭐ Texas (Dallas / Houston)
  • Legal to film police in public
  • Generally police-friendly state — courteous filming is smoother
  • Do not handle a firearm while being filmed (confusing for foreign visitors)
  • Stay calm and non-confrontational throughout
🌊 Florida (Miami)
  • Legal to film police in public spaces
  • Two-party consent law applies to PRIVATE conversations only — not police in public
  • Keep distance, do not interfere with the officer
🦅 Massachusetts (Boston)
  • Strong civil liberties state with well-established protections
  • Filming police in public explicitly protected since 2011 court ruling
  • Wiretapping law does not apply to police performing public duties

✅ Universal tips for any state

State clearly: "I am filming this interaction. I am exercising my First Amendment rights."

• Do not grab or touch the officer at any point
• Back up footage to cloud immediately
• If phone is seized: say "I do not consent to a search of my device"
• Do not delete footage if asked — this may be evidence tampering

If You Are Detained

Follow these steps in order. Stay calm throughout the entire interaction.

1

Stay calm — do not run

Do not run. Do not resist physically — even if you believe the detention is unlawful. Your safety comes first.

2

Clarify your status

Ask: "Am I being detained or am I free to go?" — This legally forces the officer to clarify your situation.

3

Invoke your rights

Say clearly: "I am invoking my right to remain silent and I want a lawyer." Then stop talking.

4

Invoke consular rights

Say: "I am a citizen of [your country]. I request you notify my consulate per the Vienna Convention."

5

Use your phone call

After arrest you have the right to a phone call. Call your embassy (see Embassies tab) or someone who can get you a lawyer.

6

Do NOT sign anything

Do not sign documents without a lawyer present. Do not agree to voluntary deportation. Do not lie about your identity — this makes things significantly worse.

⚠ If you are formally arrested

You will be taken to a police station and "booked." Bond/bail may be set — your embassy may be able to assist. Even minor arrests can affect future US visa applications — even if charges are dropped. Take every interaction seriously.

Know Before You Go

Common legal mistakes foreign fans make in the USA. Avoid all of these.

🍺
Public drinking
Illegal in most US cities outside licensed areas. NYC, LA, Dallas, and Miami all actively enforce this. Fines up to $250.
🚶
Jaywalking
Technically illegal in all states. Fines $25–$50. Rarely enforced but can escalate if you argue or resist.
🌿
Marijuana
✓ Legal: NY, CA, MA ✗ Illegal: TX, FL
Do NOT carry across state lines — federal crime regardless of state laws.
🎆
Fireworks
Illegal in many cities — especially near stadiums and in residential areas. Do not bring or light fireworks.
⚠️
Weapons laws
Vary enormously by state. Do not bring anything that could be mistaken for a weapon. Leave multi-tools and pocket knives in checked baggage.
🔊
Noise ordinances
Most cities enforce quiet hours 10pm–7am. This applies to hotel rooms and Airbnbs too — neighbours do call police.
📷
Stadium photography
Professional cameras with detachable lenses are often prohibited at FIFA venues. Check each stadium's official policy before attending.

⚠ Immigration reminder

Any arrest — even minor, even if charges are later dropped — can affect future US visa applications. The arrest record may appear on background checks for years. Treat every police interaction with maximum seriousness.
