🚨 THE AMBULANCE TRAP

A 911 ambulance averages $3,200 in the USA. Your travel insurance may NOT cover it if deemed "non-emergency."

The rule: If you can physically get into an Uber, take an Uber to Urgent Care.

Only call 911 if:

  • ✕ Unconscious or unresponsive
  • ✕ Severe chest pain or heart attack signs
  • ✕ Cannot breathe or choking
  • ✕ Major trauma or bleeding that won't stop
  • ✕ Stroke symptoms (face drooping, arm weakness, speech)

EXACT SCRIPTS

Say word for word
1
🏥 At Urgent Care
"Hi, I am an international visitor. I do not have US health insurance. I would like to know your self-pay rate before I register. I am happy to pay today by card."
2
🚑 At the ER
"I am here for [describe symptom]. I am an international visitor with no US insurance. I will need an itemized bill. I would like to speak with your financial counselor before discharge."
3
📬 Disputing a bill later
"I received this bill for [amount]. I am an international visitor and this was my first knowledge of these charges. I would like to apply for financial assistance / charity care. Can you send me the application?"
4
📋 For your travel insurance claim
"I need a complete itemized bill, all diagnosis codes (ICD-10), procedure codes (CPT), and the treating physician's NPI number for insurance reimbursement."

INSURANCE CLAIM

Step by step
1
Before you go
Photograph your insurance card and policy number. Save it offline on your phone.
2
At the facility
Always ask for an itemized receipt with diagnosis code (ICD-10) and procedure code (CPT).
3
Within 24 hours
Call your insurer's emergency line to notify them of the visit. This is critical — some policies require prior notification.
4
Documents needed
Itemized bill · ICD-10 diagnosis code · CPT procedure code · Proof of payment · Your passport
5
Submit within 90 days
Most insurers have an app or email submission. Submit all documents within 90 days of treatment.
6
If denied — appeal
Request "explanation of benefits" and appeal in writing. Most denials can be overturned.
Common denial reasons
❌ "Non-emergency"
Appeal with the treating physician's note explaining medical necessity.
❌ "Out of network"
Most travel policies cover emergencies out of network. Quote the policy section directly.
❌ "Pre-existing condition"
Read your policy — most travel policies cover acute episodes even with pre-existing conditions.
